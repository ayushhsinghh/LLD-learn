## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** The lesson experience feels authored for this product. The five-phase framework, requirement-to-responsibility reasoning, original diagrams, class explorer, complete Java, simulations, and Focus Mode create a recognizable teaching system. The homepage is less distinctive: its white canvas, divider rows, badges, and arrows are polished but category-familiar, so the product’s strongest identity appears only after opening a lesson.

**Deterministic scan:** The scanner returned zero findings for `app/page.tsx`, `app/problems/ride-sharing/page.tsx`, and `content/ride-sharing.mdx`. It found no rule violations or file-level anti-patterns. Geometry checks also found no page-level horizontal overflow at 1440 px or 390 px. Wide entity diagrams, routing diagrams, and Java code are intentionally contained by nested horizontal scrollers and are not overflow defects.

**Visual overlays:** Browser mutation was unavailable: `document.title` was getter-only and `document.createElement` was not exposed. The detector therefore did not run inside the page, and no reliable user-visible overlay is available. The zero console messages must not be treated as successful overlay evidence.

## Overall Impression

The product has a disciplined, credible learning experience and an unusually clear design-reasoning model. Its biggest opportunity is to make that model continuously visible on mobile and to make the homepage behave more like a guided curriculum than a flat catalog.

## Cognitive Load

The sampled flow has moderate cognitive load: hierarchy and grouping are strong, but chunking, minimal-choice guidance, and persistent orientation weaken on long lessons. The homepage exposes 10 primary learning destinations; the curriculum drawer repeats those 10 choices; Requirements shows eight disclosure rows; the entity inventory includes 13 types plus enums and fields. These are legitimate content, but the beginner must repeatedly decide what matters now.

## Emotional Journey

Arrival feels calm and credible. Choosing a starting point creates mild hesitation because the framework is recommended but the broader lesson order is not. Lesson entry builds confidence through a concrete promise, original illustration, and Focus Mode option. Class Design is the peak: one-class-at-a-time exploration turns complexity into mastery. On mobile, confidence falls during long requirements, entity, and code sections because the learner loses phase context. The ending provides intellectual closure, but the final lesson lacks a strong completion or next-learning moment.

## What’s Working

- The requirement → responsibility → state → behavior hierarchy is unusually clear and consistent across prose and interactions.
- The desktop three-column lesson shell combines curriculum, focused reading width, and live page TOC effectively without crowding the page.
- Responsive type, illustrations, and nested wide-content scrollers prevent page overflow at 390 px while preserving detailed diagrams and code.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Restore persistent mobile lesson orientation

**Why it matters:** The core promise is a repeatable five-phase method, but mobile removes the only persistent view of those phases during a lesson that can exceed 40,000 px in height.

**Fix:** Add a compact phase-progress control that highlights the current phase and opens direct links to Requirements, Entities, Class Design, Implementation, and Extensions. Separate this from the curriculum drawer or give the drawer clear “Lessons” and “This page” scopes.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

### [P2] Prevent the fixed Index control from covering lesson content

**Why it matters:** At 390 px the 44 px-high control occupies the lower-left reading area and can overlap captions, numbered walkthroughs, cards, and code-adjacent content.

**Fix:** Reserve layout space for it, move it into a sticky mobile rail/header, or use an edge-attached handle while preserving a 44 px touch target.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

### [P2] Make the homepage a guided curriculum on mobile

**Why it matters:** Ten primary destinations appear with limited sequencing guidance, and the complete-walkthrough rows lose their chevrons on mobile while Focus Mode retains a visibly clickable pill.

**Fix:** Preserve a compact row affordance, clarify the recommended starting path, and distinguish “quick framework review,” “first lesson,” and “deep walkthrough” without adding more visual noise.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

### [P2] Compress the densest lesson transitions

**Why it matters:** Eight question reveals followed by 20 scope bullets and a large entity taxonomy can feel like a syllabus to the beginner-to-intermediate audience.

**Fix:** Lead each region with its conclusion, cluster requirements under Actions/Rules/Errors/Boundaries, summarize before expanding, and progressively disclose the entity inventory while preserving complete content.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill`

### [P3] Demonstrate the unique teaching system earlier

**Why it matters:** The homepage promises diagrams and simulations but does not show the distinctive interaction model that makes the lessons memorable.

**Fix:** Use the comp-first workflow to introduce one restrained reasoning preview or original lesson visual rather than converting the catalog into a generic card gallery.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable delight`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan, a first-time learner:** The homepage offers many credible destinations without a clear recommended sequence. “Focus Mode” is visually prominent but its role—recommended beginner path or equal alternative—is ambiguous. The entity inventory may look like a memorization checklist instead of the result of a reasoning process.

**Casey, a distracted mobile learner:** The fixed Index button sits over the reading area, the page TOC disappears, and reopening the curriculum drawer begins at its top rather than keeping the active eighth lesson in view. Returning after an interruption requires reconstructing location from the document itself.

**The interview-cramming engineer:** Desktop scanning is excellent, but mobile lacks an efficient jump path among the five phases. The homepage does not clearly separate a ten-minute framework refresher from a 60-minute deep walkthrough, and the strongest interview-ready recap is available only near the end.

## Minor Observations

- The homepage’s section header and its right-aligned metadata wrap awkwardly on narrow screens.
- Several sidebar labels and captions use approximately 11 px type with faint color; a dedicated accessibility audit should verify contrast and zoom behavior.
- The mobile drawer source exposes two controls named “Close index,” which may create redundant screen-reader announcements.
- Four class tabs fit at 390 px but approach the minimum comfortable text size.
- The right-hand desktop TOC is one of the product’s strongest interface elements and should guide the mobile solution.

## Questions to Consider

- Is the homepage a catalog, or should it explicitly behave like a curriculum?
- If a learner is 35 minutes into Ride Sharing on a phone, what single glance shows phase, progress, and next milestone?
- Is Focus Mode the recommended beginner path or simply an alternative presentation?
- Could the homepage demonstrate one real reasoning interaction instead of only promising diagrams and simulations?
- Should the final lesson end with a completion and recap action rather than only a Previous link?
